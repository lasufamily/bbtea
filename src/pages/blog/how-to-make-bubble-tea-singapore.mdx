Making bubble tea at home starts with a brewed tea base, milk and cooked tapioca pearls. The part that needs the most attention is the pearls: different packets use different cooking methods, so follow the instructions for the product you bought.

This guide explains the order of preparation and how to adjust the finished drink to your taste. It does not assign nutrition grades or promise a particular cost per cup.

## Ingredients and equipment

For a milk tea, have these ready:

- Black tea, such as Assam or Ceylon, or another tea you enjoy with milk
- Milk or a suitable alternative
- Tapioca pearls intended for drinks
- Sugar or syrup, if wanted
- Water and ice

You will also need a saucepan, strainer, heatproof jug, a glass and either a wide straw or a spoon. Check the milk and pearl packaging for ingredients and allergens. A plant-based drink or a flavoured pearl can contain ingredients you might not expect.

Choose pearls with clear preparation instructions. Instant pearls and dried pearls are different products; do not use one packet's timing for another.

## Brew the tea

Brew the tea according to its packet, using a heatproof container. Milk and ice will dilute the flavour, so start with a tea base that tastes a little stronger than your usual plain cup. Use more tea if needed rather than leaving it to steep indefinitely, which can make the drink bitter.

Strain out the leaves or remove the bags. If you want to sweeten the tea directly, dissolve the sugar while it is warm. Allow it to cool before assembling an iced drink. Keep milk refrigerated until you need it.

## Cook the pearls

Read the pearl packet before starting. Bring the specified amount of water to the boil, add the pearls and stir gently to keep them from sticking. Cook and rest them as directed. Do not assume they are ready simply because they float.

Test a pearl once it is safe to taste. If the centre is hard, check the packet's guidance for further cooking. Drain or rinse only as instructed; some products use a resting stage in the cooking water.

If you are coating the pearls in syrup, transfer them to a small bowl and add enough syrup to coat them. Prepare only what you intend to use and follow the packaging's storage advice. Pearl texture changes as they sit, so assemble the drink soon after preparation.

## Assemble your drink

Add the prepared pearls to a glass, followed by the cooled tea. Pour in milk a little at a time and taste before adding more. Add ice once the balance is close to what you want.

Taste again before adding syrup: milk, pearl coatings and other toppings can already contribute sweetness. Stir thoroughly, since syrup left at the bottom can make the first sip very different from the last.

If you use a wide straw, make sure it fits the pearls. A spoon works too. Do not serve hard, undercooked pearls.

## Adjust the next cup

| What you notice | What to try |
| --- | --- |
| The drink tastes watery | Brew a stronger tea base or use less ice in the assembled drink. |
| The tea tastes bitter | Check the brewing temperature and steeping time on the tea packet. |
| The milk hides the tea | Add milk gradually and stop earlier. |
| It tastes too sweet | Use less syrup next time and check whether the pearls or milk alternative are sweetened. |
| Pearls have a hard centre | Check the cooking and resting instructions for that particular packet. |
| Pearls become too soft | Prepare a smaller batch and use them promptly, following the storage instructions. |

Change one part at a time so you can tell what made a difference. A note of the tea, milk and pearl product you used is more useful than trying to copy a shop's sweetness percentage: those labels are specific to each shop's recipe.

## Buying ingredients and checking prices

Compare packet sizes, preparation instructions and storage needs when shopping. A large bag is not necessarily useful if you only make an occasional drink. Retail prices change, so check current prices rather than relying on a fixed cost estimate for a homemade cup.
